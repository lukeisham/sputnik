# Transitions

Transitions are the bridges between sentences, paragraphs, and sections. They guide the reader through your argument and signal how each thought relates to what came before. Weak or missing transitions suggest unclear thinking — not just unclear writing.

## Key Rules

1. **Use Logical Connectors** — *however, therefore, in contrast, building on this, consequently, similarly, meanwhile, for instance*.
2. **Match Transition to Relationship** — Don't use *however* for an addition, or *therefore* for a contrast.
3. **Avoid Over-Signalling** — Not every sentence needs a transition word. Let parallelism and natural flow do some of the work.
4. **Transition at Boundaries** — The end of one paragraph and the start of the next form a natural hinge; use it.

✅ The experiment failed to replicate. **However**, a subsequent trial with tighter controls produced the expected result.
❌ The experiment failed to replicate. It was raining outside that day.

✅ The committee approved the budget. **Building on this**, the team launched the first phase in March.
❌ The committee approved the budget. The team launched the first phase in March. (no logical connection shown)

## Why Weak Transitions Matter

A missing or wrong transition forces the reader to guess the relationship between ideas. If they guess wrong, your argument collapses. If they must stop to figure it out, you lose their attention.
