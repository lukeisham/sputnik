# Colon Usage

The colon is a mark of announcement. It tells the reader: *what comes next explains, illustrates, or completes what came before.* A colon is stronger than a semicolon but less final than a period.

## Key Rules

1. **Introducing a List** — Use a colon after a complete sentence that introduces a list: *The kit includes three items: a compass, a flashlight, and a map.*
2. **Introducing a Quotation** — When the lead-in is a complete sentence: *Churchill's instruction was simple: "Never give in."*
3. **Explanatory Clause** — The second clause explains or amplifies the first: *The decision was unanimous: every board member voted yes.*
4. **Colon vs Semicolon** — A semicolon joins two independent clauses of equal weight. A colon joins them when the second explains, illustrates, or completes the first.

✅ She had one goal: to finish the marathon before sunset.
❌ She had one goal: and trained every day for six months.

✅ There are three options: accept the offer, negotiate, or walk away.
❌ There are three options, accept the offer, negotiate, or walk away.

## Capitalisation After a Colon

In American English, capitalise the first word after a colon if it begins a complete sentence. In British English, lowercase is standard. Both conventions agree: be consistent.
