# Comprise vs Compose

These two words describe the same relationship — part to whole — but from opposite directions. Misusing them is one of the most common errors in formal writing.

## Key Rules

1. **Comprise** — The whole *comprises* its parts. *Comprise* means *consists of, is made up of*. The whole comes first.
2. **Compose** — The parts *compose* the whole. *Compose* means *make up, constitute*. The parts come first.
3. **Never "Is Comprised Of"** — The phrase *is comprised of* is universally rejected by style guides. Use *comprises* or *is composed of*.

✅ The team comprises five members. *(whole = team; parts = five members)*
❌ Five members comprise the team.

✅ Five members compose the team. *(parts = five members; whole = team)*
❌ The team is comprised of five members.

## Memory Aid

Think of *comprise* as "comprehensive" — the whole *includes* the parts. Think of *compose* as "composer" — the parts *build up* the whole, like notes building a symphony.
