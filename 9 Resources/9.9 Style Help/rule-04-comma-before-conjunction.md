# Place a Comma Before a Conjunction Introducing a Co-ordinate Clause

When a conjunction (_and_, _but_, _for_, _or_, _nor_, _while_ in the sense of _and at the same time_) joins two independent clauses, place a comma before the conjunction.

## Key Points

- Each clause must be able to stand alone as a complete sentence.
- When the subject is the same for both clauses and the connective is _and_, the comma may be omitted if the relation is close.
- With _but_, always use the comma even when the subject is shared.

✅ The early records of the city have disappeared, and the story of its first years can no longer be reconstructed.
✅ The situation is perilous, but there is still one chance of escape.
✅ I have heard his arguments, but am still unconvinced.
✅ He has had several years' experience and is thoroughly competent.

The comma signals that the first clause is complete and that a new, co-equal clause follows.

❌ The sun was setting and the temperature began to drop. &nbsp;_(Two independent clauses — needs a comma.)_
❌ She finished her work, but was too tired to go out. &nbsp;_(This one is actually acceptable — shared subject with_ but _is fine with a comma.)_
