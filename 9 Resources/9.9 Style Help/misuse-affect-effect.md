# Affect vs. Effect

## The Distinction

*Affect* is a verb meaning *to influence*. *Effect* is a noun meaning *result* (and less commonly, a verb meaning *to bring about* or *accomplish*).

✅ The new policy will **affect** all employees.
❌ The new policy will **effect** all employees.

✅ The **effect** of the policy was immediate.
❌ The **affect** of the policy was immediate.

## Effect as a Verb

When used as a verb, *effect* means *to bring about* or *to cause to happen*:

✅ The new CEO **effected** sweeping changes.
(She brought them about — she didn't just influence them.)

## Vague Noun Uses

Strunk warns against using *effect* as a vague noun in arts criticism: "an Oriental effect," "effects in pale green," "a charming effect." If you have a definite meaning, express it directly.

❌ The painting achieves subtle effects of light and shadow.
✅ The painting captures the way morning light filters through the leaves.

## Quick Check

If you can replace the word with *influence*, use **affect** (verb).
If you can replace it with *result*, use **effect** (noun).
If you can replace it with *bring about*, use **effect** (verb).
