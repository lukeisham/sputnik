# Italics and Capitalisation for Titles

## Standard Practice

For the titles of literary works, scholarly usage prefers italics with capitalised initials.

✅ The *Iliad*; the *Odyssey*
✅ *As You Like It*; *To a Skylark*
✅ *The Newcomes*; *A Tale of Two Cities*

## Possessives Before Titles

Omit the initial *A* or *The* from a title when you place a possessive before it.

✅ Dickens's *Tale of Two Cities*
❌ Dickens's *A Tale of Two Cities*

## Publisher Variation

The usage of editors and publishers varies — some use italics with capitalised initials, others use Roman with capitalised initials and with or without quotation marks. Strunk recommends italics except when writing for a periodical that follows a different practice.

## Modern Equivalents

In plain-text environments where italics are unavailable, use quotation marks or underscores:
- `_The Great Gatsby_` or `"The Great Gatsby"`

In Markdown, use asterisks or underscores:
- `*The Great Gatsby*` renders as *The Great Gatsby*

✅ Always capitalise the principal words in titles.
✅ Use consistent formatting throughout a document.
