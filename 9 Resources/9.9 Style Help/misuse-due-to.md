# Due To

## Incorrect Adverbial Use

*Due to* is incorrectly used for *through*, *because of*, or *owing to* in adverbial phrases.

✅ He lost the first game **because of** carelessness.
❌ He lost the first game **due to** carelessness.

## Correct Adjectival Use

*Due to* is correct when related as predicate or modifier to a particular noun.

✅ This invention is **due to** Edison.
✅ Losses **due to** preventable fires are unacceptable.
✅ The cancellation was **due to** weather.

## The Test

If you can replace *due to* with *caused by* or *attributable to*, it is correct. If you need *because of*, use *because of*.

✅ The delay was **due to** a technical fault. (*caused by* works)
❌ The flight was cancelled **due to** fog. (*because of* is needed)

## Modern Note

The adverbial *due to* is now so common that many readers do not notice it. In formal writing, preserve the distinction.
