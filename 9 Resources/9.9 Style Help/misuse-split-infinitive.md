# Split Infinitive

## The Traditional View

There is precedent from the fourteenth century for placing an adverb between *to* and the infinitive, but the construction is in disfavour and is avoided by nearly all careful writers.

✅ To inquire diligently
❌ To **diligently** inquire

## The Classic Example

❌ To **boldly** go where no man has gone before.
✅ To go **boldly** where no man has gone before.

## When Splitting Is Justified

Modern usage accepts the split infinitive when the unsplit version is awkward or changes the meaning:

✅ The company plans to **more than double** its output.
(Placing "more than" elsewhere changes the meaning.)

✅ She expected the value to **almost** triple.
(Compared to "almost to triple" — which is worse.)

## Modern Consensus

Most modern style guides (including the later Strunk & White) take a moderate view: avoid the split infinitive when it is easy to do so, but do not torture a sentence to avoid it. Clarity and natural rhythm should prevail over rigid adherence to the rule.
