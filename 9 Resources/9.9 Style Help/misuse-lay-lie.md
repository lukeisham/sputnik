# Lay vs. Lie

## The Distinction

*Lay* is a transitive verb — it takes a direct object (you lay *something* down). *Lie* is intransitive — it does not take an object (you lie *yourself* down).

| | Present | Past | Past Participle | Present Participle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lay** (to place) | lay | laid | laid | laying |
| **Lie** (to recline) | lie | lay | lain | lying |

## Examples

✅ I **lay** the book on the table. (present — placing something)
✅ I **laid** the book on the table yesterday. (past)
✅ I have **laid** the book there every day. (past participle)

✅ I **lie** down for a nap. (present — reclining)
✅ I **lay** down for a nap yesterday. (past — note: *lay*, not *laid*!)
✅ I have **lain** here for an hour. (past participle)

## Common Errors

❌ I'm going to **lay** down for a while.
✅ I'm going to **lie** down for a while.

❌ He **laid** on the sofa all day.
✅ He **lay** on the sofa all day.

❌ She has **laid** there since noon.
✅ She has **lain** there since noon.

## Quick Check

If you can replace the verb with *place* or *put*, use a form of **lay**.
If you can replace it with *recline*, use a form of **lie**.
