# Between vs Among

The traditional rule is simple: *between* for two items, *among* for three or more. The reality is slightly more nuanced, but the rule remains a reliable starting point.

## Key Rules

1. **Between Two** — Use *between* when referring to a relationship involving two distinct people or things: *The secret was between Alice and Bob.*
2. **Among Three or More** — Use *among* for relationships involving three or more, especially when the group is treated as a mass: *The rumour spread among the employees.*
3. **Between for Multiple Distinct Items** — When three or more items are named individually and the relationship is one-to-one, *between* is correct: *The treaty was signed between France, Germany, and Italy.*
4. **Among for an Undifferentiated Group** — When the items are not named individually: *She could not choose among the many options.*

✅ The negotiations between the union and management lasted all night.
✅ The negotiations among the three departments lasted all night.

✅ The prize money was divided among the five winners.
❌ The prize money was divided between the five winners. *(prefer among)*

## The Exceptions

*Between* is correct for exact locations: *The village lies between the mountains, the river, and the forest.* *Between* is also correct with *and* in a fixed construction: *Choose between soup, salad, or the chef's special.* When in doubt, default to the two-vs-three-or-more rule.
