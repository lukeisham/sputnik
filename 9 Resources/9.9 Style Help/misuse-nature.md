# Nature

## Often Redundant

*Nature* is frequently unnecessary — used from habit, like *character*. Cut it when it adds nothing.

✅ Hostile acts
❌ Acts of a hostile **nature**

✅ The documents were confidential.
❌ The documents were of a confidential **nature**.

## Vague Uses

Strunk also warns against vague uses of *nature* in expressions like "a lover of nature" or "poems about nature." Unless specific statements follow, the reader cannot tell whether the poems concern natural scenery, rural life, the sunset, the untracked wilderness, or the habits of squirrels.

✅ She writes poems about the forests of the Pacific Northwest.
❌ She writes poems about **nature**.

## Legitimate Uses

*Nature* is correct when it carries precise meaning:

✅ Human **nature**; the **nature** of the problem
✅ "Nature red in tooth and claw"

## The Test

If you can delete "of a [something] nature" and the sentence still works, delete it.
