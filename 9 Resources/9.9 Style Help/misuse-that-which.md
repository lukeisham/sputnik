# That vs. Which

## The Restrictive / Non-Restrictive Rule

*That* introduces a restrictive clause — one essential to the meaning of the sentence. *Which* introduces a non-restrictive clause — one that adds information but is not essential. Non-restrictive clauses are set off by commas.

✅ The book **that** I borrowed from you is excellent.
(The clause identifies *which* book — essential.)

✅ The book, **which** I finished last night, is excellent.
(The clause adds extra information — not essential.)

## The Comma Test

If the clause is surrounded by commas, use *which*. If it isn't, use *that*.

✅ Cars **that** run on electricity are becoming common.
(Not all cars — only electric ones. Restrictive. No commas.)

✅ Electric cars, **which** produce no tailpipe emissions, are becoming common.
(All electric cars share this quality. Non-restrictive. Commas.)

## A Key Distinction

❌ The report, **that** was published yesterday, contains errors.
✅ The report, **which** was published yesterday, contains errors.

❌ The report **which** I wrote is on your desk.
✅ The report **that** I wrote is on your desk.

## British Usage Note

In British English, *which* is commonly used for both restrictive and non-restrictive clauses. In American English, the distinction is observed more strictly and is preferred in formal writing.
