# A Participial Phrase at the Beginning Must Refer to the Grammatical Subject

When a sentence opens with a participial phrase (or an adjective phrase, appositive, or prepositional phrase describing a noun), that phrase must modify the grammatical subject of the main clause. Violations are often unintentionally comic.

## Key Points

- Identify who or what performs the action in the opening phrase — that must be the subject of the sentence.
- The same rule applies to introductory adjectives, appositives, and prepositional phrases describing a person or thing.

✅ Walking slowly down the road, he saw a woman accompanied by two children.
✅ On arriving in Chicago, his friends met him at the station. _(Awkward: "his friends" didn't arrive — fix as below.)_
✅ When he arrived in Chicago, his friends met him at the station.
✅ Young and inexperienced, I thought the task easy.

❌ Walking slowly down the road, a woman accompanied by two children came into view. &nbsp;_(The woman was walking, but she's not the subject.)_
❌ Being in a dilapidated condition, I was able to buy the house very cheap. &nbsp;_(The house was dilapidated — not the buyer.)_
❌ Wondering irresolutely what to do next, the clock struck twelve.
