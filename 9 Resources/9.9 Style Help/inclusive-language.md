# Inclusive Language

Inclusive language is not a political accessory — it is a precision tool. When your language excludes or distracts a portion of your audience, your argument loses reach. And when you use a gendered term where a neutral one belongs, you risk inaccuracy as well as discourtesy.

## Key Rules

1. **Singular *They*** — When gender is unknown or irrelevant, *they* is grammatically sound and stylistically cleaner than *he or she* or *s/he*. Modern style guides endorse it.
2. **Bias-Free Occupations** — *Firefighter* not *fireman*, *chair* or *chairperson* not *chairman*, *flight attendant* not *stewardess*.
3. **Avoid Unnecessary Gendered Markers** — *Female CEO* or *male nurse* implies the role is unusual for that gender. Drop the marker unless it is relevant to the point.
4. **Check for Assumptions** — *Husband and wife, he took his wife, the nurse… she* — these assume demographics that may not match reality.

✅ Every student should bring their own laptop to the exam.
❌ Each student should bring his or her own laptop to the exam.

✅ The pilot addressed the passengers.
❌ The pilot addressed his passengers.

## Why This Is a Clarity Issue

If a reader stops to question your language, they have stopped following your argument. Inclusive language removes friction — it lets the widest possible audience engage with your ideas without distraction.
