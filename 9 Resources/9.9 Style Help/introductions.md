# Introductions

An introduction has three jobs: orient the reader in your topic, state your claim or purpose, and earn the right to keep their attention. A weak introduction loses the reader before your argument starts.

## Key Rules

1. **Orient the Reader** — Establish context without drowning in background. Answer: *What is this about, and why should I care?*
2. **State the Claim** — End the introduction with a clear thesis statement or purpose sentence. The reader should know what you are arguing by the last sentence of paragraph one.
3. **Earn Attention** — Vivid detail, a provocative question, or a surprising fact earns attention more than *"Throughout history, people have…"*.

✅ In 2023, three of the world's five largest banks lost more money to preventable human error than to market risk. This essay argues that current compliance training is structurally broken — and proposes a fix.
❌ The topic of bank compliance is very important in today's world. In this essay, I will discuss compliance training.

## The Funnel vs the Hook

The traditional "funnel" (broad → narrow) works for academic writing. The "hook" model — start with the most arresting detail and work outward — is often better for non-academic audiences. Know which one you are using.
