# Consistent Voice

Voice is the personality that comes through in writing — the sense that a specific human being, not a committee or an algorithm, is doing the talking. Finding your voice takes practice. Maintaining it across a document takes discipline.

## Key Rules

1. **Know Your Default Register** — Are you naturally formal, conversational, witty, or plainspoken? Start there and adjust for audience, but don't fake a voice you cannot sustain.
2. **Avoid Voice-Switching** — A paragraph that lurches from *"It is hereby recommended that the committee…"* to *"Honestly, just give it a shot"* breaks the reader's trust. Pick a lane.
3. **Consistency in Pronouns** — If you use *you* or *we* in one section, don't switch to *one* or passive constructions without purpose.
4. **Read Aloud** — Voice problems are easier to hear than to see. If a sentence sounds like someone else wrote it, it probably did.

## Finding Your Voice

Voice emerges from the intersection of vocabulary range, sentence rhythm, and attitude toward the subject. It cannot be forced — but it can be refined through revision, reading widely, and writing regularly.
