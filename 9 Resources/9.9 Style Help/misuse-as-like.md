# As vs. Like

## The Rule

*Like* governs nouns and pronouns. Before phrases and clauses, use *as* (or *as if*, *as though*).

✅ We spent the evening **as** in the old days.
❌ We spent the evening **like** in the old days.

✅ He thought **as** I did.
❌ He thought **like** I did.

## Nouns and Pronouns

*Like* is correct before a noun or pronoun alone:

✅ He thinks **like me**.
✅ She runs **like** the wind.

## Clauses

Before a clause (a group of words with a subject and verb), use *as*, *as if*, or *as though*:

✅ It looks **as if** it might rain.
❌ It looks **like** it might rain.

✅ Do **as** I say, not as I do.
❌ Do **like** I say.

## Modern Usage Note

In informal speech, *like* before clauses is widespread ("It feels like I've been here before"). In formal writing, the distinction still matters. When in doubt, test: if what follows contains a verb, use *as*.
