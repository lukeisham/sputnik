# Em Dash, En Dash, and Hyphen

These three horizontal marks look similar but serve distinct functions. Using the wrong one is like using a comma where a period belongs — it will not always cause confusion, but it will signal a lack of care.

## Key Rules

1. **Hyphen (-)** — Joins compound words and modifiers: *well-known author, high-speed rail, mother-in-law*. Also used for line breaks in justified text.
2. **En Dash (–)** — Marks ranges (numbers, dates, times): *pages 10–25, 1990–2000, Monday–Friday*. Also used in compounds with proper nouns: *Paris–London train*.
3. **Em Dash (—)** — Creates a strong break in thought — a parenthetical pause more emphatic than commas but less formal than parentheses. Used for interruptions, emphasis, or explanatory asides.

✅ The three candidates — two from marketing, one from engineering — presented their proposals.
❌ The three candidates - two from marketing- one from engineering- presented their proposals.

✅ The conference runs March 5–8 in the Tokyo–Osaka corridor.
❌ The conference runs March 5-8 in the Tokyo-Osaka corridor.

## Spacing Convention

In American English, no spaces around em dashes: *The answer—if there is one—remains unclear.* In British English, thin spaces or full spaces are common: *The answer — if there is one — remains unclear.* Pick one convention and stick to it.
