# Formal vs Informal

The choice between formal and informal register is not about which is "correct" — it is about which serves the audience and the purpose. A formal tone signals authority and distance. An informal tone signals approachability and connection. Using the wrong one undermines your message.

## Key Rules

1. **Contractions** — Acceptable in informal writing (*can't, won't, it's*). Avoid in formal (*cannot, will not, it is*).
2. **Second Person** — *You* is standard in instructions, blogs, and business emails. Avoid in academic papers and legal documents unless you are directly addressing the reader.
3. **Colloquialisms** — *A lot, kind of, sort of, pretty good* belong in informal contexts. Replace with specific, precise language in formal writing.
4. **Hedging Language** — *Maybe, perhaps, I think, kind of, sort of, seems like* — use sparingly. In formal writing, qualify claims with evidence, not verbal padding.

✅ *Formal:* The committee recommends that the policy be revised no later than Q3.
✅ *Informal:* We think the policy should be updated by September.

## The Decision Test

Ask: *Who is reading this, and what is at stake?* A job application, a grant proposal, and a legal brief require formality. A team update, a blog post, and a personal email do not. When in doubt, aim one step more formal than you think necessary — you can always warm it up in revision.
