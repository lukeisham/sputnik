# Case

## Avoid Unnecessary Use

*Case* means "instance of a thing's occurring" or "usual state of affairs." In these senses, the word is usually unnecessary and can be cut.

✅ Many of the rooms were poorly ventilated.
❌ In many **cases**, the rooms were poorly ventilated.

✅ Few mistakes have been made.
❌ It has rarely been the **case** that any mistake has been made.

## Legitimate Uses

*Case* has proper uses in specific contexts:

✅ In **case** of fire, use the stairs.
✅ That presents a difficult **case** for the court.
✅ The grammatical **case** of the pronoun is nominative.

## The Test

If you can delete "in many cases," "in the case of," or "it is the case that" without changing your meaning, delete them.

❌ In the **case** of the Henderson account, we need more time.
✅ The Henderson account needs more time.
