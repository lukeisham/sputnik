# Ellipsis

The ellipsis (three dots: …) serves two main purposes: indicating omitted material in a quotation, and conveying a trailing off or hesitation in dialogue or narrative. Misusing it muddies both your citations and your tone.

## Key Rules

1. **Omission in Quotations** — Use an ellipsis to show you have removed words from a quoted passage. The resulting text must remain grammatically sound and faithful to the original meaning.
2. **Trailing Off** — In dialogue or informal narrative, an ellipsis indicates a thought left unfinished or a pause: *"I was going to tell you, but…"*
3. **Four Dots at Sentence Boundary** — When an omission crosses a sentence boundary, use a period plus an ellipsis: *. …* (four dots total).
4. **Do Not Use for Simple Pauses** — A comma or a period controls pacing better than an ellipsis in formal prose. Overusing ellipses makes writing feel tentative and unfinished.

✅ Full text: "The committee met on Tuesday and, after lengthy debate, voted to approve the budget."
Quoted with omission: "The committee met on Tuesday … and voted to approve the budget."

❌ The budget was approved… and then everything changed… and nobody knew what to do…
