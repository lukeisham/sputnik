# Shall vs. Will

## The Traditional Rule

The future tense requires *shall* for the first person, *will* for the second and third.

| Person | Simple Future | Determination / Consent |
|---|---|---|
| I, we | I **shall** go | I **will** go (determination) |
| you | you **will** go | you **shall** go (command) |
| he, she, it, they | they **will** go | they **shall** go (command/obligation) |

## In Practice

✅ I **shall** arrive at noon. (simple future)
✅ I **will** not tolerate this. (determination)

✅ They **will** arrive at noon. (simple future)
✅ They **shall** comply with the regulations. (obligation)

## Conditional: Should vs. Would

The same pattern applies: first person uses *should*, second and third use *would*.

✅ I **should** not have succeeded without his help.
✅ He predicted that before long we **should** have a great surprise. (indirect quotation)

## Modern Reality

Outside the most formal British English, the *shall/will* distinction has largely collapsed. *Will* is used for all persons in ordinary speech and writing. The traditional rule is worth knowing for formal documents, legal writing, and literary analysis — but for everyday writing, *will* for all persons is accepted everywhere.
