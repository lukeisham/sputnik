# Etc. (Et Cetera)

## Meaning and Limitations

*Etc.* means *and the rest* or *and so forth*. It should not be used if these phrases would be insufficient — that is, if the reader would be left in doubt about any important particulars.

## Correct Use

*Etc.* is least objectionable when it represents:
- The last terms of a list already given in full
- Immaterial words at the end of a quotation

✅ The primary colours are red, yellow, blue, etc.
✅ Bring pens, paper, notebooks, etc.

## Incorrect Use

❌ Her speech touched on democracy, justice, etc.
(The reader cannot guess the missing items.)

❌ Some of the greatest writers — Homer, Shakespeare, etc.
(*Etc.* cheapens the list of great names.)

## Never After *Such As* or *For Example*

*Etc.* is incorrect at the end of a list introduced by *such as*, *for example*, or any similar expression — those phrases already signal an incomplete list.

❌ Many European capitals, such as Paris, London, Rome, etc.
✅ Many European capitals, such as Paris, London, and Rome.
