# While

## Avoid Indiscriminate Use

Avoid using *while* as a casual substitute for *and*, *but*, or *although*. Many writers use it to vary their connectives without regard for whether it fits the logic.

✅ The office and salesrooms are on the ground floor; the rest of the building is devoted to manufacturing.
❌ The office and salesrooms are on the ground floor, **while** the rest of the building is devoted to manufacturing.

## Correct Use: *Although*

*While* is allowable as an equivalent of *although* when it leads to no ambiguity or absurdity:

✅ **While** I admire his energy, I wish it were employed in a better cause.
(Paraphrase: I admire his energy; at the same time, I wish...)

## Incorrect Use: Simultaneity Implied

❌ **While** the temperature reaches 90 or 95 degrees in the daytime, the nights are often chilly.

This implies the heat and the chill happen at the same time — which is absurd. Use *although*:

✅ **Although** the temperature reaches 90 or 95 degrees in the daytime, the nights are often chilly.

## Best Practice

Use *while* only with strict literalness, in the sense of *during the time that*:

✅ She read a book **while** she waited.
✅ The phone rang **while** I was in the shower.

For logical contrast, prefer *although*, *but*, *whereas*, or a semicolon.
