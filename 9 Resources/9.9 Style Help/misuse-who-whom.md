# Who vs. Whom

## The Rule

*Who* is the subject form (the person doing the action). *Whom* is the object form (the person receiving the action).

✅ The candidate **who** wins the primary will face the incumbent.
(**Who** is the subject of *wins*.)

✅ The candidate **whom** the party endorsed won the election.
(**Whom** is the object of *endorsed*.)

## The He/Him Test

If you can replace the word with *he* or *she*, use **who**. If you can replace it with *him* or *her*, use **whom**.

- (**Who/Whom**) called? → **He** called. → **Who**
- (**Who/Whom**) did you call? → You called **him**. → **Whom**

## Common Trap: Parenthetical *He Said*

Do not let expressions like *he said* or *I think* mislead you about the case:

✅ His brother, **who** he said would send him the money...
❌ His brother, **whom** he said would send him the money...

(Test: **He** would send the money — so *who*, not *whom*.)

✅ The man **who** he thought was his friend...
❌ The man **whom** he thought was his friend...

(Test: **He** was his friend — so *who*, not *whom*.)

## Prepositions

After a preposition, use *whom*:

✅ To **whom** should I address the letter?
✅ The person with **whom** I spoke was very helpful.
