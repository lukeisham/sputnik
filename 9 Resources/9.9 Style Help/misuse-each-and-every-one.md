# Each, Every, and Singular Agreement

## The Rule

Words like *each*, *each one*, *everybody*, *every one*, and *many a man* are singular — even though they imply more than one person. They require singular pronouns and verbs.

✅ **Each** of the candidates **has** submitted a proposal.
❌ **Each** of the candidates **have** submitted a proposal.

✅ **Everybody** brought **his** own lunch.
❌ **Everybody** brought **their** own lunch. (See note below.)

## Common Distributive Expressions

| Singular Expression | Correct Verb | Correct Pronoun |
|---|---|---|
| each | has, is | his, her |
| each one | has, is | his, her |
| everybody | has, is | his, her |
| every one | has, is | his, her |
| many a man | has, is | his |

## Modern Singular *They*

Strunk, writing in 1919, prescribed *he* for all antecedents unless the antecedent was necessarily feminine. Today, singular *they* is widely accepted and preferred for gender-neutral writing. The grammatical agreement of the verb, however, should remain singular:

✅ Every student should bring **their** own materials. (modern, accepted)
