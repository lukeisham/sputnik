# Plain Language

The Plain English movement argues that clear writing is a form of respect — and in many contexts, a legal requirement. Governments, banks, and healthcare providers increasingly mandate plain language so that citizens can understand their rights, obligations, and options.

## Key Rules

1. **Prefer Short Words** — *Use* not *utilise; help* not *facilitate; show* not *demonstrate; need* not *requirement to effect compliance with*.
2. **Use Active Voice** — *We sent the form* not *The form was sent*.
3. **Concrete Nouns** — *Dogs, chairs, firefighters, children* are easier to process than *paradigms, apparatuses, synergies, stakeholders*.
4. **Short Sentences** — Average 15–20 words per sentence. One idea per sentence. Break complex ideas across two or three sentences instead of cramming them into one.
5. **Direct Address** — Use *you* when writing instructions or information for a specific reader.

✅ If you miss the deadline, you will lose your application. Apply now at gov.uk/benefits.
❌ Failure to submit the requisite documentation within the prescribed 28-day period shall result in the termination of the application process in accordance with statutory regulation 14(b).

## When Not to Use Plain Language

Technical precision, legal nuance, and academic rigour sometimes require complex language. The rule is not "never use long words" — it is "never use a long word when a short one does the job better."
