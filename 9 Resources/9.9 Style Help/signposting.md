# Signposting

Signposting is the explicit practice of telling the reader where you are in your argument and where you are going next. In longer documents — academic papers, reports, technical proposals — it prevents the reader from getting lost. In shorter pieces, it can feel heavy-handed.

## Key Rules

1. **Use In Longer Documents** — Anything over 3-4 pages benefits from phrases like *"This section argues…"*, *"Having established X, we now turn to…"*, *"As discussed above…"*.
2. **Keep It Brief** — *"Next, I analyse the survey data"* works; *"The subsequent portion of this manuscript shall engage with the data"* does not.
3. **Avoid Overuse** — If every paragraph begins with *"In this paragraph,"* or *"Next,"*, the signposting has become clutter.
4. **Use First-Person Sparingly** — *"I will now argue…"* is acceptable in humanities; in scientific or business writing, prefer *"The next section examines…"*.

✅ Having established the scope of the problem, we now turn to the proposed solution.
❌ In this paragraph, I am going to tell you about the solution, which I will now begin to describe.

## When to Skip

In a short memo, a blog post, or a narrative essay, signposting reads as padding. Trust your reader to follow a well-structured argument without a tour guide.
