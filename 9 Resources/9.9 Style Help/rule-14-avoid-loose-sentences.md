# Avoid a Succession of Loose Sentences

A "loose sentence" in this sense is one consisting of two co-ordinate clauses joined by a conjunction or relative pronoun. One such sentence is fine; a series of them becomes monotonous and sing-song.

## Key Points

- Vary sentence structure: mix simple sentences, periodic sentences, and sentences joined by semicolons.
- If you find a paragraph consisting mostly of _...and..._, _...but..._, _...so..._ clauses, recast enough of them to break the pattern.
- The connectives to watch for are _and_, _but_, _so_, and non-restrictive _who_, _which_, _when_, _where_, and _while_.

✅ Varied paragraph with simple, complex, and periodic sentences interwoven.
✅ A mix of sentence lengths and structures that reflects the real relations of the thought.

❌ The third concert was given last evening, and a large audience attended. Mr. Appleton was the soloist, and the Symphony Orchestra provided the music. The soloist showed himself an artist of the first rank, while the orchestra proved fully deserving of its reputation. The interest in the series has been gratifying, and it is planned to give a similar series annually. The fourth concert will be on Tuesday, when an equally attractive programme will be offered.

Recast weak loose sentences into simple sentences, two-clause sentences with semicolons, or periodic sentences — whichever best represents the thought.
