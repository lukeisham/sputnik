# Punctuating Parenthetic Expressions

## Punctuate Outside as if Parentheses Were Absent

A sentence containing a parenthetic expression is punctuated outside the parentheses exactly as if the parenthetic part were not there. The expression inside is punctuated independently — except that the final stop is omitted unless it is a question mark or exclamation point.

✅ I went to his house yesterday (my third attempt to see him), but he had left town.
❌ I went to his house yesterday, (my third attempt to see him), but he had left town.

✅ He declares (and why should we doubt his good faith?) that he is now certain of success.

## Detached Expressions

When a wholly detached expression or sentence is parenthesised, the final stop comes before the closing parenthesis.

✅ The committee reached a unanimous decision. (The chair abstained from voting.)

## Key Points

- Never place a comma, period, or semicolon before an opening parenthesis.
- If a complete sentence stands alone in parentheses, capitalise and punctuate it normally.
