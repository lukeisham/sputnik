# Respective / Respectively

## Usually Unnecessary

These words can usually be omitted with advantage. They add syllables without adding clarity.

✅ Works of fiction are listed under the names of their authors.
❌ Works of fiction are listed under the names of their **respective** authors.

## When *Respectively* Helps

In a few contexts — particularly formal or technical writing — *respectively* is needed to pair items in parallel lists:

✅ The one mile and two mile runs were won by Jones and by Cummings.
(No *respectively* needed when the pairing is obvious.)

In a technical context where ambiguity must be avoided:

✅ The values of *x*, *y*, and *z* were 3, 7, and 12, **respectively**.

## Avoid in Ordinary Writing

In writing on ordinary subjects, *respectively* rarely adds anything. Strunk considered it appropriate mainly for formal proofs (e.g., geometry).

## The Test

If the pairing is clear without *respectively*, cut it.

❌ Tom and Sarah drove a Ford and a Toyota **respectively**.
✅ Tom drove a Ford and Sarah drove a Toyota.
