# Masterful vs. Masterly

## The Distinction

*Masterful* means *domineering*, *imperious*, or *wilful*. *Masterly* means *exhibiting the skill of a master* — expert, accomplished.

✅ She gave a **masterly** performance of the concerto.
❌ She gave a **masterful** performance of the concerto.

✅ The **masterful** executive brooked no dissent.
(The executive was domineering, not merely skilful.)

## Why It Matters

If you describe a pianist, painter, or writer as *masterful*, you are saying they are domineering, not that they are skilled. The two words praise very different qualities.

## Memory Aid

- **Masterly** — think of a master craftsman: skilful, accomplished
- **Masterful** — think of a master over servants: commanding, imperious

## Modern Drift

In contemporary usage, *masterful* has absorbed the meaning of *masterly* to the point that the distinction is fading. For precise writing, especially in criticism and reviews, preserve the difference.

✅ The chef prepared a **masterly** meal.
✅ Her **masterful** personality dominated the boardroom.
