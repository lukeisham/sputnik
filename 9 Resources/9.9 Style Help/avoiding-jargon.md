# Avoiding Jargon

Jargon — specialised vocabulary shared by a professional group — is a double-edged tool. Used well, it communicates precisely and signals competence. Used poorly, it excludes readers and masks weak thinking behind impressive-sounding words.

## Key Rules

1. **Keep It When It's Precise** — If the technical term is the only accurate word for the concept (e.g., *photosynthesis*, *due diligence*, *API endpoint*), keep it. Precision matters.
2. **Replace It When It Excludes** — If a plain-English equivalent exists and your audience is non-specialist, use the plain version. *Utilise → use; leverage → make use of; paradigm shift → major change*.
3. **Define on First Use** — If a technical term is essential but may be unfamiliar, define it concisely the first time it appears.
4. **Beware the Jargon Echo Chamber** — If you cannot define a jargon term in simple words, you may not understand it yourself.

✅ The drug inhibits serotonin reuptake, increasing the neurotransmitter's availability in the synapse.
❌ The medication leverages molecular cascade synergies to optimise patient wellness outcomes.

## The Reader Test

Read the sentence and imagine a colleague outside your field. Would they understand it? Would they trust it, or would they suspect you are hiding something? If the answer is "maybe not," revise.
