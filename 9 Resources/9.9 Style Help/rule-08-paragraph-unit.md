# Make the Paragraph the Unit of Composition

A paragraph should develop a single topic. When you move to a new topic, start a new paragraph. The paragraph break is a signal to the reader that a new step in the argument or narrative has begun.

## Key Points

- One paragraph = one topic. Subdivide liberally to guide the reader.
- A single sentence is rarely a proper paragraph — the exception being sentences of transition between larger sections.
- In dialogue, each change of speaker begins a new paragraph.

For a short composition, a single paragraph may suffice. For longer works, group related ideas under logical heads. For example, a book review might use:

1. Account of the work.
2. Critical discussion.

A more detailed literary analysis might break into paragraphs covering facts of publication, metrical form, subject, treatment, distinguishing qualities, and relation to other works.

✅ Short, focused paragraphs that each develop one idea.
❌ A rambling paragraph that mixes plot summary, character analysis, and historical background in a single block.
