# Less vs. Fewer

## The Rule

*Less* refers to quantity. *Fewer* refers to number.

✅ He had **fewer** men than in the previous campaign.
❌ He had **less** men than in the previous campaign.

## The Meaning Test

✅ His troubles are **less** than mine.
(*His troubles are not so great as mine.* — quantity/degree)

✅ His troubles are **fewer** than mine.
(*His troubles are not so numerous as mine.* — number)

## Common Applications

| Use *Less* For | Use *Fewer* For |
|---|---|
| Less water | Fewer bottles |
| Less time | Fewer hours |
| Less money | Fewer dollars |
| Less traffic | Fewer cars |

## The Round-Number Exception

*Less* is correct before a round number treated as a collective unit:

✅ The signers of the petition were **less** than a hundred.
✅ The project will take **less** than three weeks.

## The Supermarket Test

The sign reading "10 items or less" is technically wrong — it should be "10 items or fewer." In informal contexts this distinction is widely ignored, but formal writing should preserve it.
