# Misuse of *Hopefully*

*Hopefully* as a sentence adverb — *"Hopefully, the rain will stop"* — is grammatically common but stylistically controversial. Traditionalists object that it changes the meaning from "I hope the rain will stop" to "the rain will stop in a hopeful manner."

## Key Rules

1. **Traditional Use** — *Hopefully* means *in a hopeful manner* and modifies the verb: *"She waited hopefully by the phone."* This use is always correct.
2. **Sentence Adverb Use** — *Hopefully* meaning *I hope that* or *it is to be hoped that* is widely understood but still draws objections in formal writing. Many style guides advise avoiding it.
3. **Alternatives** — Use *I hope that, with luck, if all goes well,* or restructure the sentence.

✅ She looked hopefully at the horizon, searching for the ship.
❌ The ship will arrive, she thought hopefully. *(ambiguous — manner or sentence adverb?)*

✅ I hope the committee reaches a decision by Friday.
❌ Hopefully, the committee will reach a decision by Friday. *(if you want to avoid the style objection)*

## When to Use It

In casual writing and speech, *hopefully* as a sentence adverb is fine — the battle is largely lost. In formal writing, academic papers, and legal documents, avoid it. The safest path: use *I hope* or restructure.
