# Very

## Use Sparingly

Use *very* sparingly. Where emphasis is necessary, use words strong in themselves.

✅ The task was **arduous**.
❌ The task was **very** difficult.

✅ She was **exhausted**.
❌ She was **very** tired.

## Why It Weakens Writing

*Very* is a qualifier that promises intensity but delivers vagueness. It tells the reader that the next word is not quite strong enough — so the writer is turning up the volume rather than choosing the right word.

## Replacements

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| very big | enormous, vast, colossal |
| very small | tiny, minute, minuscule |
| very important | crucial, vital, essential |
| very angry | furious, enraged |
| very happy | delighted, elated, overjoyed |
| very sad | despondent, heartbroken, bereft |
| very fast | rapid, swift, breakneck |
| very slow | sluggish, glacial, plodding |

## When *Very* Is Acceptable

*Very* has legitimate uses — particularly with adjectives that lack a stronger one-word equivalent, or in speech and informal writing:

✅ That is a **very** specific question. (no obvious stronger word)
✅ I was **very** pleased to meet her. (natural, unforced)
