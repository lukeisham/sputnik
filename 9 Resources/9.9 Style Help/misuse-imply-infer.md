# Imply vs Infer

These two words describe the two ends of the same communication act, but they travel in opposite directions. Confusing them suggests a weak grasp of logic, not just vocabulary.

## Key Rules

1. **Imply** — The speaker or writer *implies*. To imply is to suggest something without stating it directly.
2. **Infer** — The listener or reader *infers*. To infer is to draw a conclusion from evidence or suggestion.
3. **Direction** — Implication goes out (speaker → listener). Inference comes in (listener ← evidence).

✅ The spokesperson implied that layoffs were coming but refused to say it directly.
✅ From the tone of the memo, employees inferred that layoffs were coming.

❌ The spokesperson inferred that layoffs were coming.
❌ From the tone of the memo, employees implied that layoffs were coming.

## Memory Aid

The speaker *implies* (sends out a suggestion). The listener *infers* (draws a conclusion in). Think: *I* for incoming (infer), *E* for emitting (imply). If you are the one doing the suggesting, you are implying. If you are the one doing the detective work, you are inferring.
