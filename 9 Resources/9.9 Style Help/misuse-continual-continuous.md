# Continual vs Continuous

These two words describe repetition, but with a crucial difference: *continual* means happening repeatedly with interruptions; *continuous* means happening without interruption. Using one for the other changes whether the action ever stops.

## Key Rules

1. **Continual** — Repeated over time with breaks in between: *continual interruptions, continual complaints, continual efforts*.
2. **Continuous** — Unbroken, unceasing: *continuous rain, continuous noise, a continuous line*.
3. **The Test** — If something could still happen "on and off," it is *continual*. If it must be unbroken, it is *continuous*.

✅ The continual power outages frustrated residents. *(power goes on and off)*
❌ The continuous power outages frustrated residents. *(implies the power was always out — impossible)*

✅ The continuous hum of the air conditioner made it hard to sleep.
❌ The continual hum of the air conditioner made it hard to sleep. *(a hum that stops and starts is not really a hum)*

## Memory Aid

*Continuous* contains *continuum* — think of a single, unbroken line. *Continual* is close to *continually repeated* — think of a cycle that restarts again and again.
