# Word Division (Syllabication)

## When to Divide

If there is room at the end of a line for one or more syllables of a word — but not the whole word — divide the word. Do not divide if it cuts off only a single letter, or only two letters of a long word.

## Principles of Division

### (a) Divide by Formation
Split according to the word's structure:
✅ know-ledge (not knowl-edge)
✅ Shake-speare (not Shakes-peare)
✅ de-scribe (not des-cribe)
✅ atmo-sphere (not atmos-phere)

### (b) Divide on the Vowel
✅ edi-ble (not ed-ible)
✅ propo-sition
✅ ordi-nary
✅ classi-fi-ca-tion

### (c) Divide Between Double Letters
Unless they come at the end of the simple form:
✅ Apen-nines, Cincin-nati, refer-ring
✅ tell-ing (not tel-ling)

### (d) Silent *-ed*
Do not divide before final *-ed* if the *e* is silent:
✅ treat-ed
❌ roam-ed, nam-ed

## Consonant Combinations

✅ for-tune, pic-ture, sin-gle, presump-tuous, illus-tration, indus-try, instruc-tion, sug-ges-tion, incen-diary

## Modern Note

Modern word processors handle hyphenation automatically. This guidance is most relevant for manual typesetting or when overriding automatic hyphenation.
