# So as an Intensifier

## Avoid in Writing

In formal writing, avoid the use of *so* as an intensifier: "so good," "so warm," "so delightful." The word adds emphasis without precision.

✅ The performance was extraordinary.
❌ The performance was **so** good.

✅ The weather was oppressively hot.
❌ The weather was **so** warm.

## Legitimate Uses

*So* is correct when it introduces a clause of result (paired with *that*) or when it means *therefore*:

✅ She practised **so** diligently that she won the competition.
✅ I think, therefore I am.

## In Speech

The intensifying *so* is natural and acceptable in speech. The caution applies to formal writing, where stronger, more specific words carry more weight than *so* + adjective.

## Quick Fix

When you catch yourself writing "so [adjective]," ask: can I replace it with one stronger word?

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| so big | enormous, vast |
| so tired | exhausted |
| so happy | delighted, elated |
| so sad | despondent, heartbroken |
