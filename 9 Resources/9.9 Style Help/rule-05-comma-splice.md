# Do Not Join Independent Clauses by a Comma

If two or more grammatically complete clauses are not joined by a conjunction, use a semicolon — or make them separate sentences. A comma alone is not strong enough.

## Key Points

- The semicolon links related independent clauses without a conjunction; it signals a closer bond than a period.
- If a conjunction is present, use a comma instead (see Rule 4).
- If the second clause begins with an adverb (_accordingly_, _besides_, _then_, _therefore_, _thus_), the semicolon is still required.

✅ Stevenson's romances are entertaining; they are full of exciting adventures.
✅ It is nearly half past five; we cannot reach town before dark.
✅ Stevenson's romances are entertaining, for they are full of exciting adventures.

Exception: when clauses are very short and alike in form, a comma may suffice.

✅ Man proposes, God disposes.

❌ Stevenson's romances are entertaining, they are full of exciting adventures.
❌ It was late, we decided to leave. &nbsp;_(Comma splice — use a semicolon or period.)_
