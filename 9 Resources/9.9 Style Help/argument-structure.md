# Argument Structure

An argument is not the same as an assertion. An assertion states an opinion. An argument supports a claim with evidence and reasoning. The Toulmin model — developed by philosopher Stephen Toulmin — provides a clear framework for building and testing arguments in everyday writing.

## Key Rules

1. **Claim** — The statement you want your reader to accept.
2. **Evidence** — Facts, data, examples, or testimony that support the claim.
3. **Warrant** — The reasoning that connects the evidence to the claim. This is the step most weak arguments skip.

✅ **Claim:** Remote work improves productivity.
**Evidence:** A Stanford study of 16,000 workers found a 13% performance increase over nine months.
**Warrant:** Fewer interruptions and a quieter environment allow deeper focus, and the measured performance increase confirms the effect is real.

❌ **Claim:** Remote work is better. *(assertion only — no evidence, no warrant)*

## Spotting Weak Arguments

Ask three questions: *What is the claim? What is the evidence? Does the evidence actually support the claim, or is the writer just hoping I will not notice the gap?* If the warrant is missing, the argument is incomplete.
