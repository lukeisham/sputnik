# Fewer vs Less

The distinction between *fewer* and *less* is one of the most well-known usage rules in English — and one of the most frequently broken, even by careful writers. The rule is clear; the supermarket checkout sign (*"10 items or less"*) is wrong.

## Key Rules

1. **Fewer** — Use with countable nouns, things you can count individually: *fewer apples, fewer people, fewer complaints, fewer than ten minutes*.
2. **Less** — Use with uncountable nouns, things measured in bulk or degree: *less water, less time, less money, less traffic, less than an hour*.
3. **The Test** — Can you put a number directly in front of the noun? *Three apples* — yes → *fewer*. *Three water* — no → *less*.

✅ There were fewer applicants this year than last year.
❌ There were less applicants this year than last year.

✅ We spent less money than expected.
❌ We spent fewer money than expected. *(money is uncountable)*

## Edge Cases

Time, distance, and money can be tricky. *Less than three miles* is accepted because the distance is treated as a single quantity, not three individual miles. *Fewer than ten dollars* emphasises the count of individual dollar units; *less than ten dollars* treats the amount as a single sum. Both are defensible.
