# That vs Who (for People)

When referring to people in restrictive clauses, the correct relative pronoun is *who*, not *that*. This is not a grammatical technicality — it is about recognising the humanity of the person you are writing about.

## Key Rules

1. **Who for People** — *The woman who won the prize*, not *the woman that won the prize*.
2. **That for Things and Groups** — *The company that filed the patent, the idea that changed everything*. Groups, organisations, and animals generally take *that*.
3. **Whom for Objects of Verbs or Prepositions** — *The candidate whom we interviewed* (formal) or *the candidate we interviewed* (dropped pronoun).

✅ The researchers who published the study will present their findings next week.
❌ The researchers that published the study will present their findings next week.

✅ The team that won the championship celebrated all night. *(team = collective entity)*
✅ She is someone who always keeps her promises.

## Why It Matters

Using *that* for people reduces them to objects — it is the linguistic equivalent of pointing with your thumb instead of your finger. In formal writing, job applications, profiles, and journalism, the distinction carries weight. In casual speech, *that* for people is common but still best avoided in writing.
