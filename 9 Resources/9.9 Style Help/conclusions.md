# Conclusions

A conclusion that merely restates the introduction is a wasted opportunity. A strong conclusion answers the question the reader silently asks at the end: *So what?*

## Key Rules

1. **Answer "So What?"** — Connect your argument to a larger consequence, a real-world implication, or a next step.
2. **Include a Call to Action (When Appropriate)** — Persuasive writing ends with a clear ask: *"The board should vote to revise the policy by Q3."*
3. **Resonate, Don't Fade** — End on a strong, memorable sentence. The last words carry disproportionate weight.
4. **Avoid New Arguments** — The conclusion is not the place for fresh evidence. If it's important, it belongs in the body.

✅ This problem will not solve itself. The data is clear, the timeline is tight, and the cost of inaction is measured in lost patients. We must act now.
❌ In conclusion, this paper has discussed three major problems. Many factors contribute to this. More research is needed.

## The Echo Technique

One effective pattern: echo a key word or image from the introduction in a transformed way, showing how the journey of the argument has changed its meaning. The reader feels the circle close.
