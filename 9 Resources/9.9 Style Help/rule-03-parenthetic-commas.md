# Enclose Parenthetic Expressions Between Commas

Any word, phrase, or clause that interrupts the flow of a sentence without being essential to its meaning should be set off by commas (one on each side, or a comma and a period at the end of a sentence).

## Key Points

- A parenthetic expression is supplementary, not restrictive — removing it leaves the sentence grammatically intact.
- Never insert only one comma; parenthetic commas always come in pairs.
- Non-restrictive relative clauses (those that do not identify the antecedent) always take commas.

✅ The best way to see a country, unless you are pressed for time, is to travel on foot.
✅ The audience, which had at first been indifferent, became more and more interested.
✅ Monday, November 11, 1918.

Always treat the following as parenthetic: years in dates, _etc._, _jr._, and non-restrictive clauses.

❌ Marjorie's husband, Colonel Nelson paid us a visit yesterday.
❌ My brother you will be pleased to hear, is now in perfect health.
