# Singular *They*

## Strunk's Original Rule (1919)

A common inaccuracy is using a plural pronoun when the antecedent is a singular distributive expression — *each*, *each one*, *everybody*, *every one*, *many a man*. These require a singular pronoun.

Strunk's prescription: use *he* with all the above words, unless the antecedent is or must be feminine.

✅ Everybody should bring **his** own book. (Strunk, 1919)

## Modern Usage

Singular *they* is now widely accepted as a gender-neutral pronoun, endorsed by most major style guides including APA, MLA, Chicago, and AP.

✅ Every student should bring **their** own materials. (modern, accepted)
✅ A doctor must listen to **their** patients.
✅ Someone left **their** umbrella.

## Awkward Antecedents

Some speakers use plural *they* even with clearly singular antecedents to avoid committing to gender — or simply from habit:

❌ A friend of mine told me that **they** were moving.
✅ A friend of mine told me that **she** was moving. (if gender is known)
✅ A friend of mine told me that **they** were moving. (acceptable in modern informal usage)

## Recommendation

- **Formal writing:** Singular *they* is now acceptable; avoid "he" as a generic pronoun.
- **If it sounds awkward:** Recast to plural — "All students should bring their books."
